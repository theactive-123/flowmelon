WordPress still powers a huge share of the web, and for good reason: it’s flexible, open source and has a plugin for nearly everything. Webflow has grown from a designer’s tool into a serious platform for marketing teams. Both can run a great website. The question is which one fits the way your team works.

Editing experience

This is where most marketing teams feel the difference. In Webflow, editors change copy and images directly on the page they’re looking at, while the layout and design system stay locked. A well-built CMS means a new landing page or blog post is a form, not a ticket for a developer.

WordPress’s block editor has improved enormously, but the experience depends heavily on the theme and page builder. Two WordPress sites can feel like two completely different products to edit.

Performance and Core Web Vitals

Webflow sites ship semantic HTML, minified CSS and hosting on a global CDN by default. There’s no plugin stack adding scripts to every page. WordPress can be just as fast — but it takes a good host, a lean theme and ongoing discipline to keep it that way as plugins accumulate.

Where speed is really lostOn both platforms, the biggest performance wins come from the same place: properly sized images, fewer third-party scripts and fonts that are preloaded instead of fetched late.

Security and maintenance

WordPress’s openness is also its biggest maintenance cost. Core, theme and plugin updates need to be tested and applied, and outdated plugins remain one of the most common ways sites get compromised. Webflow is a managed platform: hosting, SSL, backups and security patches are handled for you.

“The cheapest website is rarely the one with the lowest build cost. It’s the one your team can update without calling a developer.”— FlowMelon

SEO

Both platforms can rank. Webflow gives you editable titles, meta descriptions, Open Graph tags, automatic sitemaps, clean URLs and 301 redirect management without a plugin. WordPress relies on plugins such as Yoast or Rank Math, which are excellent but add another layer to maintain. In practice, content quality and site speed matter far more than the platform.

Cost over three years

  • Webflow: predictable hosting plan per site, minimal maintenance, faster in-house updates.
  • WordPress: cheaper hosting is possible, but budget for premium plugins, updates, security monitoring and developer time.

For most marketing sites we see total cost of ownership end up close — the difference is where the money goes. With Webflow it goes into design and content instead of maintenance.

When to choose which

Choose Webflow if…

  • Your website is primarily a marketing and content engine.
  • Design quality, motion and brand consistency matter.
  • Your team wants to publish without waiting on developers.

Choose WordPress if…

  • You need deep functionality like complex memberships, forums or LMS features built in.
  • You already have a WordPress team and workflows that work well.
  • You need full server-level control over hosting.
